# scenario  runs  bounds
#
# The covariance's own promise, per scenario, asserted by `data/anees.sh` in CI (#89). Each
# scenario is flown on seeds 1..runs and `tools/anees.py` averages NEES across them at every
# epoch; the keys it prints, and why the bounds are chi-square quantiles rather than measured
# ceilings, are in its docstring. `data/scenarios.txt` asks whether one flight got less
# accurate; this asks whether the filter says how inaccurate it is, which no ceiling can: a
# ceiling passes a filter that grew more accurate and more overconfident at once.
#
# One-sided. The simulator's IMU sits one to two orders below `ImuNoise::default()`, on purpose
# (AGENTS.md), so every honest scenario is underconfident and a lower bound would fail them all.
#
# Two bounds per block, for two shapes of fault:
#
#   any_=0       no epoch past `bound_any`, the 95 % quantile made family-wise over the log's
#                epochs by Bonferroni. Valid however correlated the epochs are, so it is derived
#                rather than measured: an honest filter crosses it with probability at most 5 %.
#                It is what catches a transient -- a lockout, an adoption -- that is a sliver of
#                the log.
#   over_<=0.10  at most a tenth of epochs past `bound`, the per-epoch 95 % quantile. An honest
#                filter reads 0.05 in expectation; consecutive epochs share their error, so one
#                ensemble scatters around that, and twice the honest rate is the allowance. What
#                it catches is a mild overconfidence that never lets up and never spikes: at
#                N=50 a covariance 20 % too small puts about half the epochs past `bound`.
#
# Measured at 50 seeds on the filter these bounds were written against: every block that passes
# reads `over_` 0.000-0.032 and `any_`=0. With `ImuNoise` scaled down by ten, `harsh_imu`,
# `moving_start` and `flight` fail `any_att` (35937, 440 and 377 epochs) and `harsh_imu`
# `any_vel` (8848), while the five whose IMU is nominal still pass -- correctly, because a tenth
# of the default is still at or above that IMU's noise (the gyroscope bias walk to within 10 %),
# and `over_pos` rises only from 0.02-0.03 to 0.04-0.09 there.
#
# A scenario the filter fails today asserts the failure (`any_>=1`) on the blocks that fail, and
# gates its other blocks as usual. Each names its cause; the assertion trips when the cause is
# removed, so the line is rewritten in the diff that fixes it rather than left passing silently.
#
#   correlated       position only: every aiding error correlated, slower than
#                    `Config::correlation`'s defaults, which are the corpus's medians and
#                    understate it (`examples/simulate.rs`). (24') fuses it at too small a
#                    variance: anees_pos=1.45, over_pos=0.95, any_pos=5197. Fused as white it read
#                    anees_pos=29.16 and anees_att=3.48, over on every epoch of both. A sensor's
#                    `tau` measured offline (#51) is what removes it. #117.
#
# Three lines (24') rewrote from failing to passing, each on attitude, and only one of them for
# its cause. `moving_start` (#150) was four magnetometer headings sharing one levelling tilt error
# in the 0.2 s after the heading adoption, priced by (36') as though independent: peak_att 1.80,
# now 0.89, which is the failure itself removed. `harsh_imu` (#149) passed by a wider covariance
# rather than a smaller error, and #149 then removed the cause: an accelerometer bias fixed at
# 1.86 of a 0.1 m/s^2 prior on y, levelled in as tilt that a diagonal P0 called independent of
# the bias. The prior is now 0.2, PX4's and ArduPilot's, and (8) correlates the two. Fused as
# white, where (24') cannot cover it: any_att=2281 and peak_att 2.22 before, 214 and 1.75 at the
# wider prior alone, 0 and 1.54 with the correlation too. At the defaults peak_att 0.60 to 0.59
# and single-seed `tilt` 1.117 deg to 0.828. So this line now passes for its cause, and the
# evidence is the white run, since the line itself read the same before. `gnss_latency`'s
# attitude passed the same way, by the wider covariance alone, until (23') removed its cause below.
#
# One line (23') rewrote from failing to passing, for its cause, on all three blocks.
# `gnss_latency` fused fixes 150 ms stale as current, an error `R` does not describe:
# anees_pos=4.06 and anees_vel=1.09, any_pos and any_vel over the bound, and its attitude passing
# only by (24')'s wider covariance (any_att=1950 fused as white). Fused at the time each fix was
# taken, against the state history, it reads anees_pos=0.108, anees_vel=0.101, anees_att=0.020,
# and nothing over the bound on any epoch; fused as white too, anees_pos=0.810 and any_att=0.
#
# One line (22') rewrote from failing to passing, for its cause. `logging_dropout` refused its
# 1.2 s gap at speed and was ~7 s of rejected fixes before the recovery: anees_pos=2.81,
# peak_pos=142.2 at 61.6 s, failing all three blocks. Coasted, the state moves with the vehicle
# and the covariance grows with the gap, so the first fix is accepted: anees_pos=0.115,
# peak_pos=0.74 at the gap's end, and single-seed `pos_h_max` 23.25 m to 2.74, the error itself
# removed rather than covered. With `Config::coast` off the line fails as before.
#
# `runs=` repeats the column as an expectation, so an ensemble that lost a run breaches rather than
# being scored against the looser bound of fewer seeds.
#
# Seeds 1..runs rather than the pinned seed of `data/scenarios.txt`, which is one of them for
# most scenarios and is not special here. The departures from `mission` fly the same seeds, so
# they stay paired with it at every seed.

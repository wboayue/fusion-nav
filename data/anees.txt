# scenario  runs  bounds
#
# The covariance's own promise, per scenario, asserted by `data/anees.sh` in CI (#89). Each
# scenario is flown on seeds 1..runs and `tools/anees.py` averages NEES across them at every
# epoch; the keys it prints, and why the bounds are chi-square quantiles rather than measured
# ceilings, are in its docstring. `data/scenarios.txt` asks whether one flight got less
# accurate; this asks whether the filter says how inaccurate it is, which no ceiling can: a
# ceiling passes a filter that grew more accurate and more overconfident at once.
#
# One-sided. The simulator's IMU sits one to two orders below `ImuNoise::default()`, on purpose
# (AGENTS.md), so every honest scenario is underconfident and a lower bound would fail them all.
#
# Two bounds per block, for two shapes of fault:
#
#   any_=0       no epoch past `bound_any`, the 95 % quantile made family-wise over the log's
#                epochs by Bonferroni. Valid however correlated the epochs are, so it is derived
#                rather than measured: an honest filter crosses it with probability at most 5 %.
#                It is what catches a transient -- a lockout, an adoption -- that is a sliver of
#                the log.
#   over_<=0.10  at most a tenth of epochs past `bound`, the per-epoch 95 % quantile. An honest
#                filter reads 0.05 in expectation; consecutive epochs share their error, so one
#                ensemble scatters around that, and twice the honest rate is the allowance. What
#                it catches is a mild overconfidence that never lets up and never spikes: at
#                N=50 a covariance 20 % too small puts about half the epochs past `bound`.
#
# Measured at 50 seeds on the filter these bounds were written against: every block that passes
# reads `over_` 0.000-0.032 and `any_`=0. With `ImuNoise` scaled down by ten, `harsh_imu`,
# `moving_start` and `flight` fail `any_att` (35937, 440 and 377 epochs) and `harsh_imu`
# `any_vel` (8848), while the five whose IMU is nominal still pass -- correctly, because a tenth
# of the default is still at or above that IMU's noise (the gyroscope bias walk to within 10 %),
# and `over_pos` rises only from 0.02-0.03 to 0.04-0.09 there.
#
# A scenario the filter fails today asserts the failure (`any_>=1`) on the blocks that fail, and
# gates its other blocks as usual. Each names its cause; the assertion trips when the cause is
# removed, so the line is rewritten in the diff that fixes it rather than left passing silently.
#
#   gnss_latency     fixes 150 ms stale that `R` does not describe; #52 owns latency.
#                    anees_pos=56.39, and every block fails.
#   logging_dropout  1.2 s refused at speed, then ~7 s of rejected fixes before the recovery;
#                    #144 would coast the gap. anees_pos=10.94, peak_pos=1847.5 at 61.2 s.
#   harsh_imu        attitude only: its accelerometer bias is fixed at [0.129, -0.186, 0.081]
#                    m/s^2, 1.86 of `Initialization`'s 0.1 prior on y, and a horizontal
#                    accelerometer bias reads as tilt. Every seed shares it, so the ensemble
#                    cannot average it away. At the nominal IMU's bias the same line reads
#                    any_att=0 and peak_att 0.65 (2.22 as fixed); raising `gyro_bias_walk` to cover
#                    its gyroscope walk moves nothing. #149.
#   moving_start     attitude only: in the 0.2 s after the heading adoption at initialization and
#                    before the first GNSS velocity fusion, four magnetometer headings share one
#                    levelling tilt error that (36') prices into each `R` as though independent.
#                    peak_att 1.80 at 10.16 s; once velocity fuses it corrects that tilt and the
#                    ensemble is back under the bound. #150.
#
# Seeds 1..runs rather than the pinned seed of `data/scenarios.txt`, which is one of them for
# most scenarios and is not special here. The departures from `mission` fly the same seeds, so
# they stay paired with it at every seed.
